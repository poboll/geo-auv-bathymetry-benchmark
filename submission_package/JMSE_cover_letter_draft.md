# Cover Letter Draft for JMSE Special Issue

Dear Guest Editors,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled “Terrain-Aware Fixed-Line MBES Survey Planning from Public Bathymetric Priors: A Reproducible Benchmark and Robustness Study” for consideration in the JMSE Special Issue “Advancements in Autonomous Systems for Complex Maritime Operations”.

The manuscript addresses pre-mission fixed-line survey design for multibeam echo sounder (MBES) bathymetry, a topic aligned with the Special Issue scope on complex maritime operations and bathymetric surveying technologies. A simple lawnmower survey pattern is easy to inspect and translate into platform-specific waypoints, but uniform spacing can be poorly matched to terrain-dependent swath variation. The paper therefore develops a terrain-aware fixed-pattern survey-line benchmark that combines a local sensor-terrain footprint model, orientation search, adaptive spacing, and a small GA refinement step used only for gated local cross-track line-position cleanup.

The contribution is framed as a public-bathymetry numerical benchmark and robustness study rather than as a sea-trial or mission-log validation. The main evidence uses two GEBCO 2025 public gridded bathymetry scenes, three synthetic stress-test terrains, a supplemental four-window GEBCO scene-expansion check, an independent USGS Southern Cascadia 30 m public-grid extension, coarse-prior/fine-grid replay, structured prior-error replay, side-specific footprint validity auditing, execution-uncertainty replay, uncertainty-aware margin selection, current-drift replay, and vehicle-aware mission-time post-evaluation. The public GEBCO results show that the principal benefit is overlap discipline and layout regularization rather than dramatic route shortening: the reported hybrid layout reaches 98.99--99.55% predicted coverage, lowers mean scene-level excess-overlap violation from about 0.81% to 0.113%, and achieves a 0.75% mean scene-level path-length reduction relative to Fixed-Spacing. Across nine public windows, overlap cleanup is positive in 9/9 cases with rank-biserial effect size 1.00, while route gain is regime dependent: low-overlap public windows show modest regularization and the USGS high-complexity crop shows the strongest stress evidence, with Fixed-Spacing infeasible at about 29.96% excess overlap and the hybrid layout feasible at about 98.42% predicted coverage, 1.75% excess overlap, and about 25% path gain. The footprint audit preserves port/starboard reach asymmetry for representative layouts and finds zero changes in the declared C97/O3 feasibility decisions, while still showing local cell-count disagreement on the USGS high-complexity crop. Ablation and optimizer diagnostics show that deterministic terrain-aware spacing explains most public-scene benefit, while GA is best interpreted as a local refinement and seed-repeatability check.

We believe the manuscript fits JMSE because it contributes an auditable prior-map geometry layer for MBES survey-line planning. It also explicitly reports transfer boundaries that are important for operation-oriented readers: GEBCO is a public benchmark input rather than a survey-grade product, the hardest complex-terrain stress test remains infeasible under the single-heading lawnmower assumption, margin-aware spacing improves only moderate execution-transfer robustness, and passing the declared C97/O3 numerical gate does not imply tail-safe overlap control under stricter C99/O2 or project-specific quality thresholds. The study deliberately does not claim hydrographic-quality assurance, sea-trial validation, mission-log replay, or altitude-controlled AUV execution.

The manuscript is original, has not been published elsewhere, and is not under consideration by another journal. All authors have approved the submitted version. The manuscript-specific code, derived benchmark outputs, figure scripts, LaTeX artifacts, and SHA-256 reproducibility manifest are available in the public GitHub repository https://github.com/poboll/geo-auv-bathymetry-benchmark. The repository name is historical; the submitted manuscript frames the package as a depth-referenced MBES fixed-line planning benchmark, with altitude-aware AUV execution left for future work. The Zenodo release series is archived under concept DOI https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19919505, and the fixed archive associated with the current manuscript package is version DOI https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19919506. The raw public bathymetry sources are cited through their official GEBCO and USGS DOI landing pages.
