Dear Editors of Journal of Marine Science and Engineering,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "Terrain-Aware MBES Survey-Line Planning with Public Bathymetric Priors: A Pre-Mission Ocean-Engineering Numerical Benchmark" for consideration as an article in Journal of Marine Science and Engineering.

This manuscript presents a reproducible planning-stage benchmark for terrain-aware multibeam echo sounder (MBES) fixed-line survey planning from public bathymetric grids. The work addresses a practical marine-science-and-engineering question: when a public or project-specific bathymetric prior is available before vehicle-specific execution is designed, can terrain-dependent swath geometry improve an auditable fixed-line survey layout while preserving a reviewable planning record?

The study combines a depth-referenced footprint approximation, deterministic heading search, quantile-based adaptive spacing, gated local GA refinement, and blockwise repair for single-heading failures. The benchmark uses GEBCO 2025 public-prior windows, synthetic mechanism tests, and USGS Southern Cascadia 30 m public-grid stress windows. On the primary GEBCO scenes, terrain-aware layouts retain 98.99-99.55% predicted coverage, reduce mean excess-overlap violation from about 0.81% to 0.113%, and shorten mean path length by 0.75% relative to fixed spacing. Across nine public windows, overlap cleanup is positive in all cases. In a fixed-overlap USGS stress screen, Adaptive Spacing and the representative Hybrid layout pass all eight selected high-burden windows, whereas fixed-spacing and local fixed-width baselines pass three.

We believe the manuscript fits JMSE because it contributes a transparent marine survey-planning benchmark and keeps the evidence boundary clear: the paper evaluates planning-stage fixed-line geometry from public-prior grids, while execution-stage validation remains a separate follow-up task.

This manuscript has not been published previously and is not under consideration by any other journal.
